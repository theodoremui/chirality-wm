Findings:
- [high] Stochastic backbone defeats the unimodal output-family control (proposal.md:137-147)
  B4 retains stochastic latents. A Gaussian decoder conditional on those latents can yield a multimodal marginal predictive distribution; recurrent latents can also create temporal persistence despite independent decoder noise. Thus changing only the output head does not isolate unimodality or persistent-body uncertainty, invalidating the intended H2/H2b interpretation.
  Recommendation: Define each ablation's complete joint predictive law. Use a shared deterministic history encoder with all sampling confined to the controlled distribution module, and retain faithful stochastic B4 as a separate baseline.
- [high] Width interaction does not distinguish capacity from optimization (proposal.md:54)
  Increasing width changes optimization as well as representational capacity. A narrow model trapped at a poor stationary point under high-variance losses, while a wider model optimizes successfully, satisfies H3n and the plateau requirement without any capacity-driven information loss. Gradient norms and plateaus do not establish comparable optima.
  Recommendation: Add optimization interventions at fixed width and objective, including independent restarts, learning-rate tuning and an extended-training control. Demonstrate the proposed feature tradeoff with frozen readouts; otherwise describe H3n as width-dependent interference rather than identified capacity competition.
- [high] Memory attenuation is not evidence of information loss (proposal.md:58)
  The proposition permits z_D = r^D b, which preserves b exactly for finite D and nonzero r. A decoder trained across delays can fail because the code changes scale or orientation, even when delay-specific decoding succeeds. The specified frozen-state test does not distinguish this from forgetting or establish that the missing hold reward caused the loss.
  Recommendation: Measure probe-end recoverability and fit both shared and delay-specific readouts with matched budgets. Include an invertible contracting-code control, and restrict negative results to information usable by the tested decoder classes.
- [high] H6 confounds objective, architecture and background predictability (proposal.md:203)
  The planned B1-versus-B2 comparison changes stochastic architecture, target learning and regularization, with only moving backgrounds budgeted. A win therefore need not arise from the proposed coefficient mechanism. Moreover, the cited suite deliberately uses smoothly evolving video; task irrelevance does not imply low temporal predictability. [Distracting Control Suite](https://arxiv.org/html/2101.02722#S3.SS3)
  Recommendation: Use a shared backbone and readout in an objective-by-background-predictability factorial experiment, measuring predictability at the actual training horizon. Otherwise label H6 a descriptive model comparison and remove its mechanism and objective-choice conclusions.

  Recommendation: Specify and validate a pixel-conditioned reference, including background nuisance variables. If infeasible, label the sensor reference privileged, report raw pixel prediction risk, and remove the exact-information and Bayes-normalized claims for that tier.
