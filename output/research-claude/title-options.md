# Seven title options

The titles below are written for the final proposal (version 7). Each one names the objective: finding out whether predictive world models keep a body's hidden left–right asymmetry after an intervention reveals it, why they lose it when they do, and what prevents the loss. They differ in which part of the contribution they put first, so the best choice depends on which result the experiments deliver.

1. **Keeping the Hidden Side: How Predictive World Models Lose, and Can Retain, Asymmetric Mechanics**
   *Recommended for the proposal.* It covers both halves of the study (the diagnosis and the remedy) and uses plain words. It still fits if the remedy turns out to be no better than storing a snapshot, because "how they lose" remains the main finding.

2. **Symmetric Plans, Asymmetric Bodies: What World Models Keep When Evidence Is Brief**
   Best if the headline result is the comparison between training objectives (reconstruction, latent prediction, response supervision). The contrast in the first half is the paper's motivation in four words.

3. **Probe, Hold, Query: Testing Whether World Models Retain What an Intervention Reveals**
   Best for a benchmark-led paper, for example a datasets-and-benchmarks track or a result where standard models mostly succeed and the map of conditions is the contribution.

4. **Four Ways to Lose a Weak Side: A Diagnostic Account of Asymmetry Loss in Predictive Models**
   Best if all four mechanisms are confirmed with clean signatures. It overpromises if one or two are ruled out, in which case the number should change.

5. **Signed Beliefs for Asymmetric Bodies: Equivariant World Models That Can Commit to a Side**
   Best if the two-component belief beats SymPE-style and deterministic equivariant baselines on the energy score and on contrast skill. It puts the method first, so it needs a clear method win.

6. **When the Mirror Misleads: Symmetry Priors and Hidden Asymmetry in Learned Dynamics**
   A more memorable option for a talk or workshop version. It suits a paper whose central message is a caution to practitioners who mirror their data or build in equivariance.

7. **Which Side Is Stronger? Retaining Intervention-Revealed Asymmetry in Predictive World Models**
   Opens with the question that the benchmark answers, in the words a clinician or roboticist would use. Good for an audience outside machine learning, such as a robotics or biomechanics venue.

## Titles to avoid

- Titles with "chirality". The bodies studied here break a left–right label symmetry, which is different from geometric handedness, and a reviewer from chemistry or physics will notice.
- Titles that say a model "knows", "remembers" or "forgets". They describe the system as if it had intentions; "keeps", "retains" and "loses" say the same thing without that implication.
- "Identifiable", "causal" or "provable" in the title. The formal statements are elementary and hold under stated assumptions; the title should not suggest a stronger guarantee.
