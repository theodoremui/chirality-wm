# Seven title options

1. **Predictive Memory for Asymmetric Mechanics** — Recommended for the proposal. It names the learning problem in ordinary language and leaves the eventual empirical claim open.
2. **When Motion Looks Symmetric: Testing Mechanical Memory in World Models** — Emphasizes why a history of interactions can matter even when current observations look similar. Use only where the observation restriction is demonstrated.
3. **From Diagnostic Interaction to Future Response: Preserving Mechanical Asymmetry** — Best when the paper’s clearest contribution is its experimental sequence and evaluation method.
4. **Response-Sufficient Memory for Asymmetric Dynamics** — A more technical option if the paper defines precisely which future inputs and outcomes the memory must support. “Sufficient” must remain relative to that task family.
5. **Learning Persistent Mechanical Context in Equivariant World Models** — Appropriate if the explicit context mechanism outperforms an ordinary context baseline. It should not imply that equivariant world models themselves are new.
6. **Can World Models Retain What Interactions Reveal? A Bilateral Dynamics Benchmark** — Best for an empirical study whose main contribution is a reusable benchmark and a careful account of when existing methods succeed or fail.
7. **Signed Mechanical Memory for Action-Conditioned World Models** — Appropriate if preserving the direction of left–right differences is central to the implemented method. The introduction should explain “signed” and “action-conditioned” immediately.

Avoid “chirality” in the main title unless the final method truly studies geometric handedness beyond bilateral label exchange. Avoid “causal,” “identifiable,” “first,” or “guaranteed” unless the final evidence and assumptions support those stronger claims. Choose the final title after the locked experiments establish the paper’s contribution.
