# Independent final theory and positioning check

**Date:** 23 October 2026. **Scope:** `mathematical-notes.md`, `title-options.md`, `icml-positioning-and-selection.md`, and the referenced analytical check script. The evolving proposals were not edited. This review assesses the stated mathematics and claim boundaries; it does not validate trained models or establish novelty by literature search.

## Verdict

No substantive mathematical error was found in the stated risk decompositions, force example, reflection laws, or finite-slope argument. Their assumptions are appropriately narrower than the proposed empirical claims. Several small clarifications would prevent readers from applying the identities beyond those assumptions. The positioning document correctly treats a new architecture as optional and empirical evidence as necessary.

## Mathematical findings

| Item | Assessment | Recommended clarification |
|---|---|---|
| Distributional equivariance | Correct. `Pθ(· | RH,RQ) = R_*Pθ(· | H,Q)` transforms the complete input experiment and output law. It does not identify the two distributions with the query held fixed. | In the following explanatory sentence, use `Pθ` consistently instead of switching to unqualified `p`. State that input-history, query, and output reflections are the appropriate operators in their own spaces. |
| Gaussian and mixture transformation | Correct in substance. A linear output transform `A` maps a Gaussian to mean `Aμ` and covariance `AΣAᵀ`. Mixture components transform and may be relabeled. | “Transformed component weights” means weights associated with the corresponding transformed components; a reflection may permute weights without changing their numerical values. An explicit two-sign mixture should exchange sign labels and associated weights. |
| Polar and axial geometry | Correct: polar vectors use `S`, axial vectors use `det(S)S`, and a labeled skeleton also requires anatomical permutation. The notes correctly restrict the cart to its simpler side-swap operation. | Qualify the first force sentence as “bilateral forward scalar forces in this example.” A spatial force component is not generically reflection-even: mediolateral force reverses under the stated 3D reflection. |
| Force estimator and variance | Correct. Independent force noise gives `Var(F₊(δ)−F₊(−δ))=2σ²`, hence `Var(b̂)=σ²/(8δ²)`. | Declare `δ>0`, `b₀>0`, and `σ>0`. The stated sign-error expression assumes a positive probe magnitude and a positive magnitude parameter; a signed `δ` would require `|δ|`. |
| Optimal Gaussian sign error | Correct for equal sign priors, the two stated observations, known nonzero magnitude, and independent equal-variance Gaussian noise: `Φ(−2√2 b₀δ/σ)`. The sum of the two observations does not add sign information in this toy model. | Keep this explicitly confined to the static force example. Activation lag, nuisance uncertainty, correlated noise, or additional informative observations change the relevant reference. |
| Finite-slope parity | Correct by substitution. Replacing `(b,ξ)` by `(−b,Rξ)` reverses the input order in the numerator and multiplies by output parity, giving slope parity `−p`. | The probe pair must lie in the admissible input domain, and the complete initial/context variables must transform. The existing gait-phase warning is appropriate. |
| Squared-risk decomposition | Correct for scalar `Y`, finite second moments, deterministic `Z=E(H)`, the same query variable, and unrestricted optimal decoders. The sigma-field generated by `(Z,Q)` is contained in that generated by `(H,Q)`, so conditional-expectation orthogonality yields the identity. | If applying the formula to an entire trajectory vector, replace the square by a squared Euclidean norm or declare the weighted quadratic loss. The scalar response/impulse case needs no change. |
| Two-history reduction | Correct when the two histories are **conditionally equally likely given the merged state and the same query**. The excess is `(m₊−m₋)²/4`; unequal conditional weights `w,1−w` give `w(1−w)(m₊−m₋)²`. | Add “conditionally” to the equal-probability qualification. A query correlated with the hidden sign can change these weights or convey information itself. The final protocol should preserve its independently assigned query mechanism. |
| Log-loss identity | Correct under the stated common-reference-measure and finite expected-log-loss conditions, with unrestricted optimal density/probability predictors. Deterministic compression makes conditioning on `(H,Z,Q)` equivalent to `(H,Q)`. | The result is about oracle optimal risks, not the gap between two fitted neural losses. This limitation is already stated clearly and should remain. |
| Point masses under log loss | The warning is correct and useful. A deterministic conditional target may be singular relative to Lebesgue measure, making a continuous Bayes density optimum or an entropy difference unsuitable. | For maximum precision: a chosen smooth predictive density can sometimes be evaluated at a deterministic outcome, but its Bayes infimum may be unbounded and the stated finite-risk identity then does not apply. CRPS is well-defined for distributions with finite first moments, including point masses. |
| Sum/difference losses and shared-query supervision | Correct. Equal total/difference squared losses are twice bilateral squared loss. Reusing already measured endpoints creates no additional information. | Retain the distinction between information content, loss weighting, and altered computation/optimization; any empirical advantage must be traced to one of these concrete changes. |

## Independent numerical check

Re-ran the existing standard-library analytical script successfully. Its 1,000 randomized algebra cases passed. For its declared Gaussian toy settings, analytical sign error was `0.1192964`; the 200,000-draw estimate was `0.120385`, a discrepancy of approximately `1.50` binomial standard errors. This checks the code against the toy calculation. It supplies no trained-model result, no human result, and no empirical estimate of conditional mutual information.

## Title and positioning findings

The seven title options are appropriately conditional on what the eventual experiments establish. The warning about “chirality” is scientifically useful: anatomical label exchange and scalar bilateral imbalance alone do not demonstrate geometric chirality. “Response-sufficient” should remain a task-relative definition unless an appropriate sufficiency result is actually established; low prediction error on a finite test set does not prove universal sufficiency.

The positioning document's account of ICML criteria is supported by the [official 2026 reviewer instructions](https://icml.cc/Conferences/2026/ReviewerInstructions): soundness, presentation, significance, and originality are explicit dimensions, and the originality guidance includes new insights or improved understanding obtained by evaluating existing methods. The document accurately identifies these as 2026 guidance rather than asserting a 2027 deadline or format. This supports the possibility of an empirical contribution, not a prediction of acceptance.

The original common-rubric score of `66.6/100` matches its review table. The separate source-audit score is explicitly labeled as a different rubric; keeping the two out of a single longitudinal score series is correct.

One editorial synchronization point remains: the strategy table calls delayed-query training/rehearsal a “candidate optimization mechanism.” In the later design, matched trajectory and impulse objectives are shared across comparators, so this entry should be labeled an investigated/rejected standalone novelty claim or a shared training design. Otherwise the strategy memo could imply a stronger new-loss contribution than the final proposal intends. This is a presentation mismatch rather than a mathematical defect.

## Consequences for the final proposal

The mathematical material supports the restricted message: a declared observation protocol can expose an avoidable prediction cost when histories with different same-query response means are merged. It does not establish that current world models merge them, that every failed probe reflects information loss, or that parity structure solves retention. The final proposal should preserve those boundaries, use the independently assigned same-query comparison, and distinguish frozen learned weights from continued state inference at evaluation. No blocking theoretical correction is required before completing the proposal package.

## Resolution

The primary author incorporated these clarifications into `mathematical-notes.md`: space-specific reflection operators, associated mixture weights, forward scalar forces, positive probe/noise parameters, scalar loss, and conditional weighting of the merged histories. The strategy table now identifies delayed-query supervision as shared training design and rejects standalone loss novelty. No change was made to the numerical check results or empirical claim boundaries.

After this independent review, the primary author added and ran a separate 12-case Runge–Kutta check of the final proposal's finite-horizon impulse formula. Its result is recorded in `checks/analytic-check-results.json`; it remains an equation check rather than a trained-model experiment.
